## Structure: Strand → Archetype → Tier

7 strands, each broken into **problem archetypes** (the recurring "shape" of MTAP questions), each archetype offered at 3 difficulty tiers matching MTAP's own elimination-round tiering: **Easy → Average → Difficult**. A child unlocks Average only after a mastery threshold on Easy, same idea for Difficult — see [mastery threshold design](mtap-expansion-overview.md#mastery-thresholds--scaffolded-design-applies-per-archetype-per-grade).

A final **Mixed Trainer Track** (all strands shuffled, timed, tiered) simulates the actual elimination-round experience once the strand tracks are cleared — the "compete" use case.

## Per-archetype generation specs

Each entry: the **parametric shape** (what's fixed vs. what varies) and how Easy → Average → Difficult scaffold onto each other. These are templates for generating original questions, not real question text. Schema/checklist these must satisfy: [Question Template Spec](mtap-expansion-overview.md#question-template-spec).

### Strand 1 — Number Sense

**Place value & digit problems**
- Skeleton (Average, the reversed-digit case): "A 2-digit number has digit sum {s}. If the digits are reversed, the new number is {diff} more than the original. Find the original number."
- Params: t∈[1,8], u∈[0,9], t≠u; s=t+u, diff=9(u−t) (must be positive, i.e. u>t)
- Answer formula: original = 10t+u
- Distractors: reversed number (10u+t); digit sum alone (s); original with digits swapped-then-off-by-one
- Easy: single direct relationship, no reversal ("tens digit is twice the units digit, digits sum to 9 — find the number") — solved by testing t∈[1,9] against both constraints.
- Difficult: 3-digit, two combined relationships (hundreds = 2×tens, units = tens+3, digit sum given) — solved the same testing way, just one more digit.

**Primes, factors, multiples (GCF/LCM)**
- Skeleton: reuses Grade 4's gcf_lcm shape exactly (g×m, g×n with gcd(m,n)=1 → ask GCF or LCM), scaled to G5's larger number range (g∈[2,12], m,n∈[2,12]).
- Answer formula: GCF=g, LCM=g·m·n (since gcd(m,n)=1)
- Distractors: the other of GCF/LCM; product a·b; sum a+b
- Difficult: three numbers, LCM of all three (mirrors Grade 4's difficult tier, larger range).

**Rounding & comparing**
- Skeleton: "Order these from least to greatest: {v1}, {v2}, {v3}." where the vi are drawn from mixed representations (a decimal, a fraction, a whole number) all in a shared small range.
- Params: 3 distinct values in [1,20], at least one non-integer (decimal or simple fraction), converted to a common decimal internally for ordering.
- Answer formula: sorted list, formatted back to original representations.
- Distractors: reverse order; original input order (unsorted); one-swap-off from correct order.
- Difficult: values that round to the same whole number but differ when compared precisely (e.g. 7.49 vs 7 1/2 vs 7.5) — the "don't round before comparing" trap.

### Strand 2 — Fractions, Decimals, Ratio & Proportion

**Fraction/decimal/mixed-number operations**
- Skeleton: reuses Grade 4's `fraction_mixed_operations` shape (mixed number + fraction, unlike denominators, LCM-based add), scaled up with a 3rd term at the Difficult tier.
- Answer formula: standard LCM-based combination, reduced.
- Distractors: added straight across (num+num, denom+denom); used product of denominators unreduced; dropped the whole-number part.

**Ratio & proportion**
- Skeleton: reuses Grade 4's `ratio_proportion` shape (ratio a:b, given one part or the total, find the other/a part), scaled with a compound (3-quantity) ratio at Difficult.
- Answer formula: as Grade 4 — scale by the common factor k.
- Distractors: additive instead of multiplicative scaling; swapped part; half the total.
- Difficult: three-quantity ratio a:b:c, given the total, find one part.

**Rate problems**
- Skeleton: "{item} costs ₱{price} for {qty}. How much for {newQty}?" (unit-rate scaling), Difficult compares two differently-packaged rates ("which is the better buy").
- Answer formula: unit_price = price/qty; scaled = unit_price × newQty. Difficult: compare price/qty across two options, answer is the cheaper unit price's option.
- Distractors: scaled additively instead of multiplicatively; used the wrong item's rate; a plausible off-by-factor value.

### Strand 3 — Patterns & Algebra

**Sequence / nth-term patterns**
- Skeleton: "What is the next term in the sequence {a1}, {a2}, {a3}, {a4}, ...?" (Easy) / "What is the {n}th term of the sequence {a1}, {a2}, {a3}, ...?" (Average, uses `a1 + (n-1)d`).
- Params: a1∈[1,20], d∈[2,9] (arithmetic), n∈[10,30] for Average.
- Answer formula: a1 + (n−1)d
- Distractors: a1 + n·d (off-by-one in n); a1 + (n−1)(d+1) (wrong common difference); just a1×n.
- Difficult: figural pattern given as a small table of (position, count) pairs following a quadratic-free arithmetic rule — same nth-term formula, framed visually instead of as a bare number list.

**Missing-number equations / simple algebra**
- Skeleton: "A number, {op1} by {k1}, then {op2} by {k2}, equals {result}. What is the number?" (Average, two-step, invert in reverse order).
- Params: op1/op2 ∈ {doubled, plus k, minus k}; k1,k2 chosen so the number solves to a positive integer.
- Answer formula: work backward from result through op2⁻¹ then op1⁻¹.
- Distractors: applied inverses in the wrong order; used the forward operation instead of its inverse; off-by-k1 or off-by-k2 alone.
- Easy: one-step version of the same mechanic. Difficult: same two-step algebra wrapped in a short story the child must first translate into the equation.

### Strand 4 — Geometry & Measurement

**Perimeter, area, volume**
- Skeleton: reuses Grade 3/4's rectangle perimeter/area shape, extended to a composite figure (two rectangles forming an L-shape) at Average, and a reverse (given area, find missing side) at Difficult.
- Answer formula: sum of component areas/perimeters; reverse via division.
- Distractors: forgot to add/subtract the overlapping edge; used only one component shape; swapped perimeter/area formula.

**Angles**
- Skeleton: reuses the (now-fixed) Grade 4 `angles` shape — supplementary/complementary ratio-split at Easy/Average — plus a clock-hand angle at an exact hour at Difficult (`|30H − 5.5M|` with M=0, i.e. `min(30H, 360−30H)`).
- Answer formula: as Grade 4, with explicit `max`/`min` on the sampled ratio pair (the Grade 4 rescale's bug fix is reused here from the start, not rediscovered).
- Distractors: gave the smaller angle; used 90 instead of 180; for the clock case, used the hour hand's naive position instead of accounting for it being exactly on the hour mark.

**Unit conversion chains**
- Skeleton: "Convert {value} {fromUnit} to {toUnit}." single-step (Easy, cm↔m or g↔kg); two-step chain (Average, mm→m→km); combined with an area/perimeter computation (Difficult).
- Answer formula: value × conversion_factor (chained product of factors for Average/Difficult).
- Distractors: used only one factor of the chain; multiplied instead of divided (or vice versa); off by a factor of 10 (single decimal-place slip).

### Strand 5 — Statistics

**Reading/interpreting data**
- Skeleton: a small embedded data table (4-5 categories with counts) rendered as text; "How many {category}?" (Easy); "How many more {catA} than {catB}?" (Average); "If {catA} and {catB} are combined, do they exceed {catC}?" — requires reading two values and comparing (Difficult).
- Answer formula: direct lookup (Easy); subtraction (Average); sum-then-compare (Difficult).
- Distractors: looked up the wrong category; added instead of subtracted; compared without combining first.

**Averages**
- Skeleton: matches the existing worked pattern for this grade — direct average (Easy); reverse-solve for a missing value given the average (Average); discard-and-recompute when values are removed from the set (Difficult, the real confirmed MTAP-5 pattern: "average of N numbers is A; two numbers, x and y, are removed; find the new average").
- Answer formula: Easy: sum/count. Average: missing = average×count − sum(known). Difficult: new_average = (average×N − x − y) / (N−2).
- Distractors: forgot to adjust the count when removing values; used the old average unchanged; averaged only the removed values instead of the remaining set.

### Strand 6 — Classic Word-Problem Archetypes

**Age problems**
- Skeleton: the doc's own worked example above (Average tier) is canonical; Easy is the direct-relationship version without the "in N years" shift; Difficult holds the ratio across two different time points simultaneously (sum of current ages given, plus a future ratio).
- Distractors: as in the worked example — forgot to add years before dividing; subtracted instead of dividing; wrong sign on the time shift.

**Consecutive-number problems**
- Skeleton: reuses Grade 3/4's `consecutive_integer_sum` shape (sum of n consecutive integers = S, find them), extended to a *product* framing at Difficult ("the product of two consecutive integers is {P}; find the larger one").
- Answer formula: Easy/Average via Gauss pairing (n = 2S/(2a+n−1) solved for the starting integer); Difficult via testing integers near √P.
- Distractors: off by one integer in the sequence; gave the smallest instead of a specific position; used the off-by-a-small-amount distractor pattern from Grade 3's own fix (never `int(sqrt(...))`, which is structurally always correct).

**Coin/money problems**
- Skeleton: reuses Grade 3's `coin_problems` shape (two denominations, total value + total count, "alternate elimination and retention"), extended to three denominations with a count constraint at Difficult (e.g. "twice as many ₱5 coins as ₱10 coins").
- Answer formula: as Grade 3, algebraic solve; Difficult substitutes the constraint to reduce to two unknowns first.
- Distractors: found the other denomination's count instead of what was asked; divided total value by the wrong denomination; assumed an even split.

**Clock problems**
- Skeleton: elapsed time between two given readings (Easy, plain subtraction across the 12-hour wrap); angle at an exact hour (Average, `min(30H, 360−30H)`); angle at an arbitrary time (Difficult, `|30H − 5.5M|`, then `min(that, 360−that)`).
- Answer formula: as above.
- Distractors: forgot the 12-hour wraparound; used the hour hand's position without the minute-hand offset (Difficult); used 6M instead of 5.5M for the minute hand's degrees-per-minute.

**Lever/balance problems**
- Skeleton: reuses Grade 3's `lever_balance` shape (w1×d1 = w2×d2, solve for the unknown weight or distance), extended to three weights on one side vs. one on the other at Difficult.
- Answer formula: direct ratio solve; Difficult sums the known side first.
- Distractors: swapped which side is unknown; forgot to multiply by distance (used weight alone); summed instead of applying the product relationship.

**Mixture problems**
- Skeleton: "{qtyA} liters of {itemA} at ₱{priceA}/L is mixed with {qtyB} liters of {itemB} at ₱{priceB}/L. What is the price per liter of the mixture?" (Easy/Average, direct weighted average); Difficult inverts — given the target mixture price, solve for one unknown quantity.
- Answer formula: (qtyA×priceA + qtyB×priceB) / (qtyA+qtyB); Difficult solves the same equation for the unknown quantity algebraically.
- Distractors: unweighted (plain) average of the two prices; used only one component's price; swapped which quantity is unknown.

**Motion/rate problems**
- Skeleton: single mover, solve for one of speed/distance/time (Easy); two movers starting together heading toward each other, find meeting time via combined closing speed (Average); one mover with a head start, find when the second catches up (Difficult).
- Answer formula: d=st (Easy); meeting_time = distance/(speedA+speedB) (Average); catch-up_time = head_start_distance/(speedB−speedA) (Difficult).
- Distractors: used only one mover's speed; added times instead of using the closing-speed relationship; ignored the head start.

**Work problems**
- Skeleton: single worker's rate → time for a job (Easy); two workers' combined rate, classic "pipe fills a tank" (Average); one worker leaves partway through, find total time (Difficult).
- Answer formula: combined_rate = 1/tA + 1/tB, time = 1/combined_rate (Average); Difficult splits the job into a completed-together portion and a solo-finish portion.
- Distractors: averaged the two times instead of combining rates; used only the faster worker's rate; forgot to account for the partial work already done before the leave-partway-through point.

*(Basic probability was removed from Strand 5 — see note above. If probability content is wanted for the Grade 5 pack anyway, it should ship explicitly labeled as bonus/non-MTAP content, not presented as authentic MTAP-5 material.)*

### Strand 7 — Mixed Trainer Track
- Not a new archetype set — draws from the above, weighted toward Easy/Average with a minority of Difficult items, timed, matching the real elimination round's tiering feel (majority easy, fewer average, fewest difficult).

## Worked example {#worked-example}

Strand 6, Age Problems, Average tier — full trace through the [Question Template Spec](mtap-expansion-overview.md#question-template-spec), illustrating the mechanics, not a bank entry.

```
Skeleton: "{name} is {a} years old. In {years} years, {name} will be {mult}
           times as old as {other} is now. How old is {other} now?"

Params:   a ∈ [10, 16], years ∈ [3, 8], mult ∈ [2, 3] (must not equal 1)
Constraint: other_age = (a + years) / mult must be a positive integer
            → generator solves for `years` last, picking the smallest valid
              value ≥3 that keeps other_age an integer, so params never
              produce a broken question.

Answer formula:  other_now = (a + years) / mult

Distractor formulas (each a named plausible slip):
  d1 = a / mult                      // forgot to add `years` before dividing
  d2 = (a + years) - mult            // subtracted instead of dividing
  d3 = (a - years) / mult            // wrong sign on the time shift

Scaffold note: relies on the Easy-tier skill of computing "age in N years"
               (a + years) directly — Average adds the "divide by a ratio"
               step on top of that already-mastered piece.
```

Feed this skeleton + constraints + formulas to a generator (script or LLM prompt) and it can produce unlimited distinct, pre-verified Age-Problems/Average questions — each one automatically satisfying the checklist, because the checklist is baked into the template's constraints rather than checked question-by-question after the fact.

## Technique assignments

Per the [overview's Technique Library](mtap-expansion-overview.md#technique-library--named-reusable-shortcut-methods) — every archetype's fastest known method, or an honest `null` where none exists.

| Archetype | Technique | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Place value & digit problems | `null` | No faster-than-systematic method identified for reversed-digit relationship problems — solved via direct algebra or constrained trial. |
| Primes, factors, multiples (GCF/LCM) | Digit-sum divisibility rules | Quick factor elimination before full factorization. |
| Rounding & comparing | `null` | Direct rounding procedure; no shortcut beyond it. |
| Fraction/decimal ops | `null` | Standard procedure. |
| Ratio & proportion | By proportionately | Build one ratio "unit," scale to the total. |
| Rate problems | By proportionately | Same family as ratio. |
| Sequence / nth-term patterns | Arithmetic nth-term formula (`a₁ + (n−1)d`) | Not in the shared library (too narrow/standard to warrant a full entry there) — avoids listing every term to reach the nth. |
| Missing-number equations | Working backward through a chain | Invert each operation in reverse order. |
| Perimeter, area, volume | `null` (Difference-of-squares shortcut for ring/composite-difference sub-cases only) | Most sub-cases are direct formula application. |
| Angles | Clock-angle formula (`\|30H−5.5M\|`) for the clock-hand sub-case; `null` otherwise | |
| Unit conversion chains | `null` | Systematic multiplication chain. |
| Reading/interpreting data | `null` | Direct lookup/computation. |
| Averages | Using a base | Assume a uniform value, correct only the deviation — matches this strand's own confirmed "discard-and-recompute" pattern. |
| Age problems | Twice-bigger/twice-smaller (when sum+difference framed) or Alternate elimination and retention | Two valid techniques depending on exact phrasing; either is faster than full substitution. |
| Consecutive-number problems | Gauss pairing | Directly sourced from this exact archetype's real citation. |
| Coin problems | Alternate elimination and retention | Directly sourced from this exact archetype's real citation (via the Grade 3/4 anchor this strand is derived from). |
| Clock problems | Clock-angle formula | |
| Lever/balance problems | `null` | The balance equation IS the direct method; no faster shortcut beyond setting it up correctly. |
| Mixture problems | Alternate elimination and retention | Assume uniform concentration, correct for the difference. |
| Motion/rate problems | `null` generally; relative-speed/closing-distance framing for two-mover sub-cases | Two-mover problems benefit from thinking in terms of the *closing* rate rather than each mover separately, though this isn't a named technique in the library. |
| Work problems | `null` generally; Using a base for reverse-solve sub-cases | Combined-rate setup is the direct method for most tiers. |

## What's still open

- **All 21 archetypes now have full parametric templates AND a generated, hand-verified 560-question batch (2026-09-09)** — see "Per-archetype generation specs" above and `content/mtap-grade5-scaled-batch.json`. Generated at the target volume directly (9/archetype/tier, 63 slots) rather than starting small and rescaling later, applying every lesson learned from Grade 3/4's generation runs from the start.
- A first checkpoint run (2/archetype/tier, 126 questions) caught one real hang: `rounding_comparing`'s Difficult tier built its three values in strictly ascending order (`v1 < v2 < v3`) and displayed them in that same construction order, so the "original (unsorted) order" distractor was *structurally always identical* to the correct least-to-greatest answer — the exact same bug class as Grade 3's `int(sqrt(...))` distractor and Grade 4's angle-ratio bug, just a new instance of it. Fixed by shuffling the display order of the three (label, value) pairs before building the question and its distractors, so the "already in order" collision became probabilistic (and redraws when it happens) instead of guaranteed.
- The scale-up run to the full 9/archetype/tier target then surfaced a second real bug: `unit_conversion`'s Average tier (mm→km) could draw a value as small as 1000mm, whose correct answer (0.001km) got rounded by the batch's 2-decimal-place formatter down to a blank "0" — a technically-not-wrong rounding that nonetheless displays a misleading, contentless answer. Fixed by raising the mm value's floor so the km result never rounds below 0.01. Both fixes verified programmatically across the full 560-question batch: zero mismatches on `angles/average`'s (already-known-correct, carried over from the Grade 4 fix) ratio formula, and zero `unit_conversion/average` answers equal to "0".
- Two genuine natural-cap shortfalls at the 9/tier target, both structural (curated/narrow domains), not bugs: `place_value_digit/difficult` maxes out at 3 (its digit-relationship constraints — hundreds = 2×tens, units = tens+3, all digits ≤9 — only admit 4 possible tens-digit values in the first place, and one of those doesn't clear the distractor-distinctness check); `work_problems/average` comes in 1 short of target (a minor natural cap from its LCM-based divisibility constraint). Stable across 3 independent runs — same 560 total, same two shortfalls, every time.
- Not yet imported into the database, no `MTAP_GRADE5_STRANDS` in `lib/mtapContent.ts`, no `sec_packs` Grade 5 row.
