## Two corrections this doc makes to the earlier research

1. **No Ratio & Proportion strand.** The overview's very first table claimed Grade 3 had this (✅), but that claim was never backed by a quote — checked across 3 real G3 documents, none contained formal ratio notation (a:b). One source explicitly confirmed the absence. What genuinely exists at G3 is **multiplicative comparison** ("six times as many chickens as ducks") — a related but simpler archetype, included below as its own item rather than folded into a "Ratio" strand it doesn't belong to.
2. **No Age Problems archetype.** An early extraction had labeled a quote as an "age problem," but re-reading the actual quote ("Jeffry spent P12 more than Arvin, total P178") shows it's a straightforward money/difference problem with no age content at all — a mislabel, not a real archetype. Excluded here rather than carried forward on a label that doesn't match its own content.

## Structure: Strand → Archetype → Tier

6 strands (independently derived for this grade — genuinely fewer than Grade 4/5/6's 7, since neither Ratio & Proportion nor a confirmed classic-canon Age archetype exist at this grade), each broken into problem archetypes at 3 difficulty tiers (Easy → Average → Difficult), mastery-gated per the [threshold design in the overview](mtap-expansion-overview.md#mastery-thresholds--scaffolded-design-applies-per-archetype-per-grade). A capstone Mixed Trainer Track caps the grade.

## Per-archetype generation specs

Each entry: the parametric shape and how Easy → Average → Difficult scaffold onto each other. Schema/checklist: [Question Template Spec](mtap-expansion-overview.md#question-template-spec).

### Strand 1 — Number Sense

**Place value, expanded form & decomposition**
- Easy: state the place value of a single digit in a number.
- Average: fill in a missing term of an expanded-form equation (real confirmed pattern).
- Difficult: reverse — given the expanded form, reconstruct the original number, or find a missing digit that makes an expanded-form equation true.

**Roman numerals**
- Easy: convert a single Roman numeral to/from Arabic form.
- Average: perform one arithmetic operation (add/subtract) on two Roman numerals, answer in Arabic form.
- Difficult: multiply two Roman numerals (real confirmed "XCVII × XLI" pattern).

**Order of operations & estimation**
- Easy: a single two-step expression, standard order of operations.
- Average: a three-step expression combining a sum/difference with a product/quotient (real confirmed pattern).
- Difficult: an estimation problem where rounding changes which operation should be estimated first.

**Prime numbers & Goldbach-style puzzles**
- Easy: identify whether a given number is prime.
- Average: count primes within a range (real confirmed "primes between 40 and 50").
- Difficult: Goldbach-style — express an even number as a sum of two primes, or find prime factorization with exponents.

**Digit-relationship puzzles**
- Easy: a two-digit number defined by one direct relationship between its digits.
- Average: same, but requires testing a small set of candidates rather than solving directly.
- Difficult: a three-digit version, or two combined relationships.

**Division & remainders**
- Easy: direct division with remainder.
- Average: a grouping word problem (real confirmed "51 desks, 9 per row" pattern) — find how many full groups and the leftover.
- Difficult: reverse — find the largest/smallest number in a range satisfying a stated remainder condition (real confirmed pattern).

### Strand 2 — Fractions & Decimals

**Fraction operations**
- Easy: add/subtract fractions with like denominators.
- Average: unlike denominators, or a fraction-of-a-fraction (real confirmed "one half of three-fifths of 60").
- Difficult: mixed numbers requiring conversion before operating.

**Fraction of a number / of a total**
- Easy: direct "fraction of a whole number" computation.
- Average: a fraction of a group where the answer must be expressed as a fraction itself (real confirmed "two of seven girls" pattern).
- Difficult: successive fractions taken from a shrinking total (real confirmed "gave 2/5 to cousins and 1/3 to friend" pattern — each fraction applies to what remains, not the original total).

**Decimal comparison**
- Easy: compare two decimals directly.
- Average: compare a decimal to a fraction (real confirmed "48.8 kg vs 48½ kg" pattern) — requires converting one form to the other first.
- Difficult: order three or more mixed decimal/fraction values.

### Strand 3 — Patterns & Algebra

**Arithmetic sequences**
- Easy: find the next term of a simple arithmetic sequence.
- Average: find a specific N-th term without listing every term.
- Difficult: a sequence with a two-step or non-obvious rule.

**Function-machine puzzles**
- Easy: one hidden operation, solve for the output given the input.
- Average: a chain of 2-3 hidden operations, solve for the input given the output (reverse the chain).
- Difficult: two different inputs through the same machine, with the rule itself needing to be inferred from both input-output pairs before a third can be solved.

**Equation-solving (single unknown)**
- Easy: one-step equation.
- Average: multi-term addition/subtraction equation (real confirmed pattern: "2,910 + 6,151 + N = 120,316").
- Difficult: the unknown appears combined with a multi-step operation, or within a word problem requiring the equation to be set up first.

**Multiplicative comparison**
- Easy: direct "N times as many" statement, find the larger quantity.
- Average: given the larger quantity and the multiplier, find the smaller (reverse of Easy).
- Difficult: find the *difference* between the two quantities rather than either quantity directly (real confirmed "how many more chickens than ducks" framing) — requires computing both quantities as an intermediate step, not just one.

### Strand 4 — Geometry & Measurement

**Perimeter & area, including reverse problems**
- Easy: direct perimeter or area of a rectangle.
- Average: reverse problem — given the area or perimeter and one dimension, find the other (real confirmed pattern, both directions).
- Difficult: perimeter combined with a per-unit cost (real confirmed "fence it if meter costs P18.50" pattern) — a genuine two-step chain: geometry first, then a money computation on the result.

**Symmetry & polygon vocabulary**
- Easy: count lines of symmetry in a common shape (square, rectangle, equilateral triangle).
- Average: name a polygon given its number of sides (real confirmed "eleven sides" pattern), including less-common names (nonagon, decagon, hendecagon).
- Difficult: combine both — identify lines of symmetry in a named polygon that isn't one of the common shapes.

**Time & elapsed time**
- Easy: read a clock face, or compute elapsed time between two given times.
- Average: reverse — given a start time and elapsed duration, find the end time, or vice versa (real confirmed "movie lasted 2h15m, finished at 4:05pm, when did it start" pattern).
- Difficult: a repeated-duration problem across multiple days (real confirmed "70 minutes daily... in a week" pattern) — requires recognizing the multiplicative structure on top of the time-arithmetic.

### Strand 5 — Statistics

**Averages**
- Easy: direct average of a small list (real confirmed 5-value pattern).
- Average: reverse-solve — given the average and all-but-one value, find the missing value.
- Difficult: a value is added or removed and the average changes — solve for the new average or the changed value (matches Grade 5's confirmed Difficult-tier pattern, appropriate as a stretch goal here since it's not independently G3-confirmed — flagged as an extension, not a direct citation).

### Strand 6 — Classic Word-Problem Archetypes

**Coin problems**
- Easy: single denomination, direct count × value.
- Average: two denominations, total count and total value given, solve for one denomination's count (real confirmed "Isabel" pattern — see worked example below).
- Difficult: three denominations, or a stated relationship between counts (e.g. "twice as many 5-peso coins as 1-peso coins").

**Consecutive-integer-sum problems**
- Easy: sum of a short run of consecutive integers (e.g. 1 to 10), computed directly or via pairing.
- Average: sum of a longer run (real confirmed "first 50 integers" pattern) — requires the Gauss-pairing insight to be efficient, not just brute-force addition.
- Difficult: given the sum, find how many consecutive integers (starting from 1, or from a stated first term) were added.

**Money/shopping problems**
- Easy: total cost of a small multi-item purchase.
- Average: purchase plus change from a stated payment (real confirmed "P200 bill, rice and fish, how much left" pattern).
- Difficult: a purchase requiring a unit-price computation first (e.g. price per dozen given, buying a different quantity).

## Worked example

Strand 6, Coin Problems, Average tier — chosen because it has the strongest, most literal real-quote anchor of any Grade 3 archetype (fetched as raw text, not summarized).

```
Skeleton: "{name} had ₱{d1}-peso and ₱{d2}-peso coins in her bag totaling
           ₱{V}. If she had a total of {T} coins, how many of these are
           ₱{d2} coins?"

Params:   d1 = 1 (fixed — the smaller Philippine coin denomination),
          d2 ∈ {5, 10}, T ∈ {9, 11, 13, 15} (ODD ONLY — see collision note),
          x ∈ [2, T−2] (the count of ₱{d2} coins — drawn directly, not solved for)
Constraint: solve forward — pick x first, then compute
            V = d2×x + d1×(T−x), so the question always has a clean,
            guaranteed-valid answer. Both coin types are present in every
            draw (x ≥ 2 and T−x ≥ 2).

Answer formula:  x = (V − d1×T) / (d2 − d1)

Distractor formulas (each a named plausible slip):
  e1 = T − x                    // found the count of the OTHER denomination
                                 // (the ₱1 coins) instead of what was asked
  e2 = V / d2                   // naively divided total value by the bigger
                                 // denomination, ignoring the mix entirely
  e3 = T / 2                    // assumed an even split between the two
                                 // denominations

CAUGHT IN ACCURACY PASS (same category as two earlier bugs in this pack,
and a second issue found while checking it thoroughly rather than stopping
at the first fix):

1. If x were allowed to equal T/2 exactly (an even split), THREE options
   would collide at once — e1 (T−x) would equal the correct answer x, AND
   e3 (T/2) would also equal both of them. Not a rare fluke: the direct
   consequence of x = T−x whenever x = T/2. Fixed structurally by requiring
   T to be ODD, so T/2 is never an integer — same approach as the q≠1 fix
   in the Grade 4 doc and the n1≠n2 fix in the Grade 6 doc.

2. e2 (=V/d2) equals the correct answer only at the boundary x=T, already
   excluded by the [2,T−2] range — that part is clean. BUT checking e2
   against e3 (not just against the correct answer — every pair needs
   checking, not just "does it match the right answer") turned up a real
   coincidental collision: with d1=1, d2=10, T=9, x=4 → V = 10(4)+1(5) = 45,
   giving e2 = 45/10 = 4.5 and e3 = 9/2 = 4.5 — two DIFFERENT distractor
   formulas landing on the identical wrong value for that specific draw.
   Neither equals the correct answer (4), so a child couldn't accidentally
   "solve" the question this way, but two visually-identical wrong options
   in a 4-choice question is still a real quality defect. Unlike the T/2
   case, this isn't a clean algebraic identity across all draws — it's a
   coincidence for specific parameter combinations, which is exactly the
   category checklist item 11's mandatory PER-DRAW distinctness check
   (not just per-archetype structural exclusion) exists to catch. This one
   is left to that runtime check rather than an added structural constraint,
   because no single clean exclusion rule (like "T odd") removes it without
   testing further specific cases — a good concrete illustration of why the
   checklist needs both a structural layer AND a per-draw layer, not one or
   the other.

Scaffold note: relies on the Easy-tier skill of direct count×value
               computation for a single denomination — Average adds the
               "two unknowns, one total" system-setup step, solved here via
               the same forward-then-reverse logic used throughout this
               pack's templates (Grade 5's Age Problems, Grade 4's Custom
               Operation, Grade 6's Combined Average).
```
