## Strand 1 — Number Sense

### Place value, order of operations, comparison
**Technique:** Completing the whole / "dagdag-bawas"

**Easy (15s):** What is the place value of the digit 2 in 2824?

*Options: 2, 2000, 20, 200*

**Answer: 20**  
The digit 2 is in the tens place, so its value is 2 x 10 = 20.

**Average (30s):** What number is equal to 9x100 + 2x10 + 7?

*Options: 1027, 729, 972, 927*

**Answer: 927**  
9x100=900, 2x10=20, plus 7. Total = 927.

**Difficult (60s):** What number is equal to 5x1000 + 7x100 + 7x10 + 6?

*Options: 5767, 5766, 5776, 7576*

**Answer: 5776**  
5x1000=5000, 7x100=700, 7x10=70, plus 6. Total = 5776.

*Options: 37, 36, 39, 38*

*Options: CXLIX, CL, CXXXIX, CLIX*

149 decomposes into place-value blocks, giving CXLIX using subtractive notation where needed.

### Calendar/day-of-week patterns
**Technique:** Modular arithmetic (mod 7)

**Easy (15s):** Today is Thursday. What day of the week will it be 5 days from now?

*Options: Thursday, Monday, Wednesday, Tuesday*

**Answer: Tuesday**  
5 mod 7 = 5, so count 5 days forward from Thursday to reach Tuesday.

**Average (30s):** The 1st of February 1996 falls on a Thursday. Which day of the week occurs 5 times that month?

*Options: Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Wednesday*

**Answer: Thursday**  
February 1996 has 29 days starting on Thursday, so Thursday occurs 5 times.

**Difficult (60s):** The 28th of a certain month falls on a Friday. What day of the week is the 1st of that month?

*Options: Saturday, Tuesday, Friday, Monday*

**Answer: Saturday**  
The 28th is 27 days after the 1st. 27 mod 7 = 6, so count back 6 day(s) from Friday to reach Saturday.

Converting all four to decimals and comparing shows 8/9 is the largest.

**Technique:** _(none — direct computation is already the fastest method)_

**Easy (15s):** What is 1/5 of 15?

*Options: 3, 12, 6, 14*

**Answer: 3**  
15 / 5 = 3 per part, so 1/5 of 15 = 1 x 3 = 3.

**Average (30s):** How many more is 1/3 of 33 than 2/5 of 25?

1/3 of 33 = 11. 2/5 of 25 = 10. Difference = 1.

**Difficult (60s):** 24 mangoes are shared: 3/12 go to Group A and 4/12 go to Group B. How many mangoes are left over?

## Strand 3 — Ratio & Proportion

### Ratio scaling
**Technique:** By proportionately

**Easy (15s):** The ratio of red to blue marbles is 7:2. If there are 35 red marbles, how many blue marbles are there?

*Options: 35, 10, 45, 17*

**Answer: 10**  
35/7 = 5 marbles per ratio unit, so blue = 2 x 5 = 10.

**Average (30s):** The ratio of oranges to apples is 6:4. If there are 30 fruits in all, how many are oranges?

*Options: 21, 30, 12, 18*

**Answer: 18**  
30 / (6+4) = 3 fruits per unit, so oranges = 6 x 3 = 18.

**Difficult (60s):** Two numbers are in the ratio 7:2. The bigger number is 35 more than the smaller. What is the bigger number?

*Options: 49, 14, 35, 54*

**Answer: 49**  
The difference 35 corresponds to 5 ratio units, so 1 unit = 7. Bigger = 7 x 7 = 49.

## Strand 4 — Patterns & Algebra

### Arithmetic sequences
**Technique:** Arithmetic nth-term formula

**Easy (15s):** What is the next number in the pattern 11, 16, 21, ___?

*Options: 22, 25, 26, 31*

**Answer: 26**  
Each term increases by 5, so the next term is 21 + 5 = 26.

**Average (30s):** An arithmetic sequence starts at 14 and increases by 5 each time. What is the 14th term?

*Options: 196, 74, 84, 79*

**Answer: 79**  
The nth term = start + step x (n-1) = 14 + 5 x 13 = 79.

**Difficult (60s):** This pattern alternates between adding two different amounts: 10, 14, 20, 24, 30, ___. What comes next?

*Options: 34, 35, 36, 28*

**Answer: 34**  
The pattern alternates +4, +6. The last step used was +6, so the next is +4: 30 + 4 = 34.

### Two-unknowns sum/difference puzzles
**Technique:** Twice-bigger/twice-smaller from sum and difference

**Easy (15s):** The sum of two numbers is 17 and their difference is 7. What is the larger number?

*Options: 12, 5, 17, 7*

**Answer: 12**  
Larger = (sum + difference)/2 = (17+7)/2 = 12.

**Average (30s):** If you add 13 to my number and then multiply the sum by 2, you get 44. What is my number?

*Options: 31, 22, 9, 75*

**Answer: 9**  
Work backward: 44 / 2 = 22, then subtract 13: 9.

**Difficult (60s):** The product of 12 and another number is 120. What is the other number?

*Options: 12, 108, 10, 11*

**Answer: 10**  
Other number = 120 / 12 = 10.

### Simultaneous-constraint puzzles
**Technique:** Alternate elimination and retention

**Easy (15s):** There are 8 coins worth 5 or 2 pesos each, totaling 19 pesos. How many 5-peso coins are there?

*Options: 1, 8, 0, 7*

**Answer: 1**  
Trying counts by trial: 1 coins of 5 pesos and 7 of 2 pesos gives 19 total, so the answer is 1.

**Average (30s):** There are 15 tables in a snack bar. Some seat 6 people, some seat 4. If a maximum of 84 people can be seated, how many 6-people tables are there?

*Options: 12, 15, 13, 3*

**Answer: 12**  
Assume all 15 tables seat 4: that's 60 people, 24 short. Each swap to a 6-seat table adds 2 more, so 24/2 = 12 tables seat 6.

**Difficult (60s):** A piggy bank has 22 coins worth 10, 5, or 1 pesos each, totaling 53 pesos. If there are 1 5-peso coins and 18 1-peso coins, how many 10-peso coins are there?

*Options: 18, 1, 3, 4*

**Answer: 3**  
Total coins (22) minus the 1 5-peso coins and 18 1-peso coins leaves 3 10-peso coins.

## Strand 5 — Geometry & Measurement

### Area & perimeter
**Technique:** _(none — direct computation is already the fastest method)_

**Easy (15s):** What is the perimeter of a rectangle with width 6 cm and length 12 cm?

*Options: 38, 36, 18, 72*

**Answer: 36**  
Perimeter = 2 x (length + width) = 2 x (12+6) = 36 cm.

**Average (30s):** A square garden has sides of 24 m. Fencing costs 21 pesos per meter. How much does fencing the whole garden cost?

*Options: 504, 12096, 117, 2016*

**Answer: 2016**  
Perimeter = 4 x 24 = 96 m. Cost = 96 x 21 = 2016 pesos.

**Difficult (60s):** A rectangle has area 72 sq cm and length 6 cm. It is divided into two parts along its length; one part has width 7 cm. What is that part's area?

*Options: 30, 42, 6, 84*

**Answer: 42**  
The whole width is 72/6 = 12 cm. This part's width is 7 cm, so its area = 7 x 6 = 42 sq cm.

## Strand 6 — Statistics

### Averages
**Technique:** Using a base

**Easy (15s):** What is the average of 42, 38, 58?

*Options: 138, 46, 47, 58*

**Answer: 46**  
(42+38+58) / 3 = 138/3 = 46.

**Average (30s):** The average of 4 numbers is 40. Three of the numbers are 37, 30, 41. What is the fourth number?

*Options: 40, 52, 56, 160*

**Answer: 52**  
4 numbers averaging 40 sum to 160. Known numbers sum to 108, so the missing one is 160 - 108 = 52.

**Difficult (60s):** The average of the numbers 38, 36, 15, 39 is 32. If 38 is removed, what is the new average of the remaining 3 numbers?

*Options: 90, 30, 32, 31*

**Answer: 30**  
Removing 38 leaves a total of 128 - 38 = 90, and 90/3 = 30.

## Strand 7 — Classic Word-Problem Archetypes

### Age problems
**Technique:** _(none — direct computation is already the fastest method)_

**Easy (15s):** In 10 years, Alex will be 2 times Alex's current age. How old is Alex now?

*Options: 10, 5, 12, 8*

**Answer: 10**  
Let Alex's age now be a. In 10 years, Alex will be a + 10, and that equals 2 Ã— a. So a + 10 = 2a, which means 10 = a Ã— (2 âˆ’ 1), so a = 10 Ã· 1 = 10.

**Average (30s):** Marco is 7 years old now. In how many years will Marco be 3 times as old as Marco is right now?

*Options: 21, 10, 4, 14*

**Answer: 14**  
Marco is 7 now. In y years, Marco will be 7 + y, and that should equal 3 Ã— 7. So y = 7 Ã— (3 âˆ’ 1) = 14 years.

**Difficult (60s):** Marco is currently 5 times as old as Isa. In 9 years, Marco will be 2 times as old as Isa will be then. How old is Marco now?

*Options: 6, 60, 15, 24*

**Answer: 15**  
Let Isa's age now be b. Then Marco's age now is 5b. In 9 years, Marco will be 5b + 9 and Isa will be b + 9, and Marco = 2 Ã— Isa at that point: 5b + 9 = 2(b + 9). Solving gives b = 3, so Marco is now 5 Ã— 3 = 15.

### Coin problems
**Technique:** Alternate elimination and retention (higher tiers)

**Easy (15s):** Marco has 19 10-peso coins. How much money does he have in all?

*Options: 190, 200, 10, 29*

19 coins x 10 pesos each = 190 pesos.

**Average (30s):** Isa changed half of a 1000-peso bill into 10-peso coins and the other half into 5-peso coins. How many 10-peso coins did she get?

**Answer: 50**  
Half of 1000 is 500 pesos. 500 / 10 = 50 10-peso coins.

**Difficult (60s):** A jar has 14 coins worth 10, 5, or 1 peso each, totaling 67 pesos. If there are 2 five-peso coins and 7 one-peso coins, how many ten-peso coins are there?

*Options: 14, 7, 2, 5*

**Answer: 5**  
Total coins (14) minus 2 five-peso and 7 one-peso coins leaves 5 ten-peso coins.

### Position/counting-in-a-line problems
**Technique:** _(none — direct computation is already the fastest method)_

**Easy (15s):** Nina is 20th in line, and there are 3 people behind her. How many people are in line in total?

*Options: 23, 20, 22, 3*

**Answer: 23**  
Total = position (20) + number behind (3) = 23.

### Fractional-spending-chain problems
**Technique:** Working backward through a chain

**Easy (15s):** Kaye had 40 pesos. She spent 1/5 of it. How much money does she have left?

*Options: 32, 24, 8, 40*

**Answer: 32**  
She spent 40/5 = 8, leaving 40 - 8 = 32 pesos.

**Average (30s):** Bea had 24 pesos. She spent half of it, then spent 1/3 of what she had left. How much does she have now?

*Options: 8, 20, 24, 12*

**Answer: 8**  
After spending half: 12 pesos left. After spending 1/3 of that: 12 - 4 = 8 pesos.

**Difficult (60s):** Adam had 120 pesos. He spent half, then 1/3 of what was left, then 1/4 of what was left after that. How much does he have now?

*Options: 60, 120, 40, 30*

**Answer: 30**  
After each step: 60, then 40, then 30 pesos left.
