### ENGLISH
**Term 1:** Listening and Reading (EN4LR-I): Listening to and reading enumeration-description texts about mealtimes and birthday celebrations; identifying the topic and listing details in the order presented; decoding multisyllabic words with common affixes; reading grade-level text with appropriate rate, accuracy, and expression.; Speaking and Writing (EN4SW-I): Composing an enumeration-description paragraph about a family mealtime or birthday tradition; writing a friendly letter of excuse or invitation using correct parts (heading, salutation, body, closing); using vocabulary and simple/compound sentences appropriate to the topic.; Viewing and Representing (EN4VR-I): Interpreting photos, menus, or invitation cards related to mealtimes and celebrations; identifying the purpose of visual design choices in an invitation.

**Term 2:** Wk11-12: Multiplication. Multiplying 3- to 4-digit numbers by a 1-digit number, and 2- to 3-digit numbers by 2-digit numbers, with and without regrouping, for products up to 1,000,000; estimating products.; Wk13-14: Division. Representing situations using a number sentence; performing two or more operations by applying the MDAS rules; solving multi-step word problems involving one or more of the four operations, including money.; Wk16-17: Converting units. Converting units of length (m/cm, km/m), mass (kg/g, g/mg), and capacity (L/mL) from larger to smaller and vice versa; converting time measures (seconds to years); solving problems involving unit conversion, including elapsed time in hours and minutes.; Wk18-20: Similar fractions. Identifying proper fractions, improper fractions, and mixed numbers; rewriting improper fractions as mixed numbers and vice versa; plotting fractions on a number line; adding and subtracting similar fractions (including mixed numbers, and a whole number with a fraction).
**Term 3:** Wk21-22: Dissimilar and equivalent fractions. ### SCIENCE
**Term 1:** Wk1-2: Science inventions. Using secondary sources to identify a famous Filipino and/or foreign scientist and their invention/s; identifying a science invention from home or the local community and explaining its impact on everyday life.; Wk3-4: Chemical properties of materials. Describing chemical properties of materials — that they can be burnt, react with other materials, or are degradable or biodegradable; describing changes in properties of materials when exposed to temperature changes, such as burning wood or coal.; Wk5-6: Handling materials safely. Demonstrating ways to minimize harmful changes in materials, such as restricting the burning of waste materials and taking care when handling reactive materials.; Wk7-8: Gathering scientific information. Identifying issues and concerns in the local community and how they could be addressed by science, such as waste treatment; applying science process skills (grouping, classifying, communicating, open-mindedness) in a guided survey about environmental issues.; Wk9-10: Term project. Creating a simple model, illustration, or story about a favorite science invention used in everyday life, or planning and producing a sample of useful fertilizer from household waste.
**Term 2:** Wk11-12: Body systems. Describing in simple terms how the muscular, skeletal, digestive, circulatory, and respiratory systems work in animals and humans.; Wk13-14: Plant systems. Observing the root and shoot system in plants and describing why they are important.; Wk15-16: Habitats. Classifying Philippine animals and plants based on habitat — terrestrial (land), aquatic (water), or aerial (air); listing examples of animals and plants in a particular habitat such as a garden, rice field, seashore, or mangrove swamp.; Wk17-18: Life cycles. Using flow charts to compare the different stages in the life cycle of animals such as a butterfly, frog, chicken, and human.; Wk19-20: Food chains. Grouping animals according to the food they eat — herbivores, carnivores, and omnivores; drawing a simple food chain using living things from the Philippines and labeling them by feeding group.
**Term 3:** Wk21-22: Forces and movement. Discovering and predicting how rigid and soft objects can be moved and/or changed in shape; measuring distance and time when things move using simple equipment; identifying that how far an object moves in a given time is called speed; constructing and labeling simple speed graphs.; Wk23-24: Pushes, pulls, and shape. Demonstrating that pushes and pulls change the speed and direction of an object (faster, slower, turning, stopping); determining how forces change the shape of objects (pushed, pulled, stretched, bent, twisted, squeezed).; Wk25-26: Magnets. Investigating the properties of magnets, including how they affect other magnets and objects made of different materials; identifying examples of objects affecting each other without contact, such as magnets attracting objects, sunlight affecting eyes and skin, and loud noises affecting ears.; Wk27-28: Sound, light, and heat energy. Identifying that energy is something that can cause change, including light, sound, and heat energy; observing and identifying sources and uses of light, sound, and heat energy at school, at home, and in the local community.; Wk29-30: Term project. Developing a poster showing sources and uses of heat energy at home or in the neighborhood, or collaborating on a safety guide explaining how to stay safe around intense light and sound.; Wk31-32: Soils. Comparing different types of soil — sandy, clay, silt, and loam — including comparing their ability to hold water; recognizing water as a basic need of plants and animals.; Wk33-34: Soil and plant growth. Investigating the effect of different types of soil on the growth of plants.; Wk35-36: Weather instruments. Identifying basic characteristics used to describe weather — air temperature, air pressure, wind speed, wind direction, humidity, rain, and cloud cover; using weather instruments to measure and record weather characteristics during a school day.; Wk37-38: Reading and interpreting weather. Examining a local weather chart to make simple interpretations about local weather and how it might change; describing and practicing safety precautions during poor or extreme weather conditions.; Wk39-40: The Sun. Describing overall characteristics of the Sun — its composition, size, and the energy it radiates; describing changes in the direction and length of shadows to infer why the Sun changes position during a day; explaining the Sun's importance to living things.
