## CURRICULUM — GRADE 3 BOW

### COMPUTER
**Term 1:** Wk1-2: Identifying the computer and its parts. Naming the main parts of a desktop or laptop (monitor, keyboard, mouse/trackpad, CPU/system unit, speakers) and describing the job of each in simple terms; distinguishing input devices (keyboard, mouse) from output devices (monitor, speakers, printer).; Wk3-4: Proper care, posture, and safety. Practicing correct sitting posture, screen distance, and break habits to protect the eyes and body during computer use; handling hardware gently (no food/drinks near the keyboard, clean hands, no pulling cables); powering a computer on and off using the correct steps, including logging in and out safely.; Wk5-7: Mouse and keyboard control. Practicing accurate single-click, double-click, right-click, and click-and-drag with the mouse to open, move, and resize windows and icons; identifying and correctly using letter keys, number keys, spacebar, enter, backspace, delete, shift, and arrow keys; opening and closing a simple application using an icon or menu.; Wk8-10: Building basic typing accuracy. Practicing correct finger placement on the home row keys; typing one's own name, short words, and simple sentences with increasing accuracy; term project — following a short set of on-screen instructions from opening a program to closing it correctly.
**Term 2:** Wk11-13: Introduction to a word-processing or drawing program. Opening a word-processing or drawing application; typing and editing short text (own name, a sentence, a short list) — using backspace/delete to correct mistakes; using the toolbar to change font size, font color, and to bold or underline text.; Wk14-16: Drawing and simple formatting. Using basic drawing tools (pencil/brush, shapes, fill color, eraser) to create a simple labeled picture (e.g., a picture of one's family or favorite animal with a caption); adding a short typed caption or title to a drawing.; Wk17-18: Saving and opening files, with adult guidance. Saving a file with a sensible file name into a designated folder, with help; reopening a previously saved file to continue or review work; understanding why saving work regularly is important.; Wk19-20: Combining skills into a small project. Creating one labeled drawing or one short typed paragraph (about a topic of choice, e.g., "My Family" or "My Favorite Animal") that combines typing, formatting, and saving skills learned this term; presenting the finished work to a partner or the class.
**Term 3:** Wk21-22: What is the internet? Describing, in simple terms, what the internet is used for (finding information, watching videos, talking to family) and how a device connects to it; recognizing common icons for a web browser and a search engine.; Wk23-25: Staying safe online. Identifying the difference between a safe, kid-friendly website (visited with an adult) and content that is not appropriate; understanding the rule of never sharing personal information (full name, address, school, password) online without a trusted adult's permission; recognizing that not everything seen online is true, and asking a trusted adult when unsure.; Wk26-27: Simple keyboard shortcuts and habits. Learning a small set of useful shortcuts (e.g., using arrow keys to move a cursor, using backspace to fix typing) that speed up basic computer use; practicing closing a program and shutting down a computer correctly and safely.; Wk28-30: Term and year-end integration project. Planning and creating a simple multimedia output that combines a labeled drawing and a short typed paragraph on a topic of the learner's choice (e.g., a favorite place or hobby), following safe-use habits practiced throughout the year, and presenting it to the class.

### MATHEMATICS
**Term 1:** Representing numbers up to 10,000 using pictorial models and numerals; reading and writing numbers up to 10,000 in numerals and in words; Determining the place value of a digit in a 4-digit number, the value of a digit, and the digit given its place value; rounding numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, or thousand; Comparing numbers up to 10,000 using =, >, and <; ordering numbers up to 10,000 from smallest to largest and vice versa; describing the position of objects using ordinal numbers up to 100th; Illustrating and estimating the area of a square or rectangle using square tile units; exploring inductively the derivation of the area formulas for a square and a rectangle; Finding the areas of squares and rectangles in sq. cm and sq. m; solving problems involving areas of squares and rectangles; Recognizing and drawing a point, line, line segment, and ray using models; recognizing and drawing parallel, intersecting, and perpendicular lines
**Term 2:** Measuring mass in grams, kilograms, and/or milligrams using appropriate tools; estimating and comparing masses of objects, including using a balance scale; Measuring capacity in liters and/or milliliters using appropriate tools; estimating and comparing capacities of two containers; Reading and writing money in words and using Philippine currency symbols (₱ and PhP) up to ₱10,000, and the centavo sign; Adding numbers with sums up to 10,000, with and without regrouping; estimating the sum of addends with up to 4 digits; solving addition word problems up to 10,000, including problems involving money; Subtracting numbers where both numbers are less than 10,000, with and without regrouping; estimating the difference of two numbers of up to 4 digits; performing addition and subtraction of 3 to 4 numbers of up to 2 digits, observing correct order of operations, including problems involving money; Collecting data from experiments with a small number of possible outcomes (e.g. rolling a die, tossing a coin); presenting data in tables and single bar graphs (horizontal and vertical)
**Term 3:** Determining the missing term(s) in, and generating, patterns with repeating and increasing or repeating and decreasing components; Illustrating division through equal jumps on the number line and as the inverse of multiplication; dividing numbers using the 6, 7, 8, and 9 multiplication tables; finding the missing number in a multiplication or division number sentence; Dividing 2- to 4-digit numbers with and without remainder (2- to 3-digit numbers by a 1-digit number without remainder; 2-digit numbers by a 1-digit number with remainder; 2- to 4-digit numbers by 10, 100, and 1000); estimating quotients by first rounding the divisor and dividend to the nearest multiple of 10; Solving 1- to 2-step division problems involving 2- to 3-digit numbers by a 1-digit number, including problems involving money; Representing fractions that are equal to one and greater than one using models; adding and subtracting similar fractions using models; Describing and drawing the effect of a two-direction multi-step slide (translation) in basic shapes and figures

### SCIENCE
**Term 1:** Science in our daily life — identifying objects, activities, or natural events in the local environment that can be explained by science; Science processes — participating in guided science activities by asking questions and tinkering with materials; describing the uses of science equipment and materials (ruler, hand lens, scissors, balloons, modeling clay, cardboard); Describing science process skills used in simple activities — observing, predicting, and measuring using units such as millimeter, centimeter, and meter; Describing the physical properties of solid materials (hard, shiny, or stretchable); Explaining that changes in materials can be harmful to living and non-living things in the environment (e.g. trash disposal, burning household materials); Demonstrating proper handling and disposal of materials according to their properties (reusing objects, disposing of excess oil into garbage, recycling paper, plastic, or glass)
**Term 2:** Using the skills of observing, predicting, and measuring in performing simple guided science activities; Observing and describing the difference between living and non-living things, giving examples of each found in the local environment; Describing the characteristics of living things — they grow, respond, and reproduce; Observing and describing the outer body parts of animals (head, legs, wings) and identifying their role in moving and gathering food; Observing the outer parts of plants (leaves, roots, stems) and identifying their role in getting water and nutrients from the soil; Identifying the basic needs of all living things (air, food, water, shelter); explaining how living things depend on one another and the environment to meet these needs; recognizing the need to protect and conserve the environment
**Term 3:** Participating in guided activities to locate and describe different types of non-living things found in and around the school (rocks, soil, water, air, metals, clouds, rain, sunlight); Identifying useful things people have made from non-living materials and the natural materials used to make them; recognizing that non-living materials making up the environment are called "earth materials"; Observing and recording changes in the weather during a day or over several days; describing the different types and patterns of weather that occur in the local area; Describing how changes in the weather can affect daily activities and explaining how some types of weather can be dangerous for people; Participating in guided activities to carefully observe and describe the natural objects commonly seen in the sky during daytime and nighttime, including the Sun, the Moon, planets, and stars; Exploring and recording how and when the Sun, the Moon, planets, and stars can be seen moving across the sky
